Drill 0029 — Layering a Temporary Override Over Authored Content

Answer before expanding. Source: lesson 0029, issue #156.

1. Two obvious ways to make every NPC go to the plaza temporarily are to rewrite their schedules, or to add a mode flag and branch the movement code. Give the specific failure of each.
Answer Rewriting schedules turns read-only authored data into mutable state. Restore now requires remembering exactly what you changed, per NPC, and any path that skips the undo corrupts authored content permanently. A mode flag duplicates the walk — one copy for schedules, one for gathering. The copies drift, and the newer one is always worse, because the older one has years of edge cases beaten into it.
2. What single property makes the nullable-override approach cheap to undo?
Answer Nothing was ever overwritten. The authored schedule was out-ranked, not suspended, so gatherTarget_.reset() is the entire restore and it needs no memory of the previous state. If your restore needs to know what was there before, you built the schedule-rewriting version.
3. While a resident is walking to the plaza, their nameplate still reads "baking bread". Bug or intended? Justify.
Answer Intended, and it is the visible consequence of "out-ranked, not suspended". As far as her authored day is concerned she is baking bread; she has been pulled away from it. Suppressing the label would mean the override had replaced the schedule rather than outranking it.
4. The original guard was if (timeOfDayHours < 0.f || schedule_.empty() || state_ != NpcState::Idle) break;. Which of the three conditions must still stop an override, and why are the other two different in kind?
Answer state_ != NpcState::Idle must still break: it means something else owns this NPC's movement (combat, death, an arrest), and an override must not fight it. This is also why dead NPCs need no separate check anywhere in the walk. The other two only mean "there is no authored answer right now", which an override does not care about — splitting them out is what lets a resident with no schedule at all still gather.
5. Splitting that guard required re-wrapping the activity_ assignment in if (hasSchedule). What would have broken otherwise, and what class of bug is that?
Answer The old code returned before touching activity_, so a clockless or scheduleless NPC kept its last label. The restructured code reaches the assignment, so it would have started clearing it. That is an unrelated behaviour change smuggled in under a movement fix — the kind that surfaces months later as "why do nameplates go blank in the sandbox?"
6. World::setPlazaGather(false) clears the override on dead NPCs too, even though dead NPCs were never given one on the way in. Why is the asymmetry correct?
Answer Because a resident can die while gathered. They were alive when slots were handed out, so they hold a destination; the state_ != Idle guard just stops them acting on it. "Released except the corpses" is exactly how someone turns up standing in the plaza the next morning — or the moment anything revives them.
7. The issue says "release on the Resolution → Investigation rollover". Why is releasing on leaving the Vote phase a real bug rather than an equivalent phrasing?
Answer The reveal happens in Resolution, the phase immediately after the vote. Releasing on exit from Vote empties the plaza at the exact moment the scene pays off. The two phrasings differ by precisely the phase the whole feature exists to stage.
8. Why key the release on the phase being entered (to == Investigation || matchEnded) rather than on dayAdvanced?
Answer dayAdvanced is correct for the ordinary rollover but misses a match that ends straight out of the vote, and any future path back to Investigation. Keying on entry makes the release total; clearing when nobody is gathered is a harmless no-op, so the release cannot be missed by a transition nobody has written yet.
9. MatchClock::endMatch() got a doc comment out of this issue. What trap does it warn about?
Answer It returns no PhaseTransition. The plaza release fires off the transition that advance() returns, so a future early-exit feature that calls endMatch() directly — "host quits", "someone wins early" — would skip the release and strand the town in the plaza. Either route the exit through the frame loop, or release at the new call site.
10. Why can the gather point not simply be zoneCentre("plaza")?
Answer Gus's hot dog cart is a solid collider at x ∈ [-3, 3], z ∈ [-2, 2], and the plaza's computed centre is (0, 0) — inside it. Sending the town to the geometric centre walks all of them into a wall. A geometric centre is a statement about bounds, not about emptiness.
11. The codebase had been reporting this obstruction for months. Where, and what is the transferable habit?
Answer [llm_npc] mystery: no clear spot in zone "plaza" — body placed at the zone centre, which may be inside a building, logged by Mystery.cpp. The habit: when a spatial default "obviously" works, read what is actually authored at that coordinate — and treat a long-standing warning nobody chased as evidence rather than noise.
12. The ring deliberately does not re-pack when a resident dies. Why is the gap a feature?
Answer The vote scene exists so a player can see who is still alive and who is missing. A ring that closes up hides exactly the information the scene was staged to show. Residents keep whatever slot they were given; the hole is the signal.
13. A test fixture placed residents at (-70, -52), inside the bakery's collider, and the regression test "an NPC with no override moves exactly as before" still passed. Why is that passing test worthless, and what is the general rule?
Answer Both the before and after cases were pinned in place by a wall, so the test compared "did not move" with "did not move" and proved nothing. A movement test that never moves always passes. When asserting that something travels, assert the travel first — a fixture that cannot move makes every downstream claim vacuous.
14. Name the three properties that identify this pattern in an unfamiliar codebase.
Answer (1) The override is nullable, so absence is representable and is the default. (2) It is consulted at the single point the authored value is read, not branched around the consumer. (3) Restore is a reset, not a replay.
15. The issue's economy note said "if this needs more than one new field on the NPC, the approach is wrong." What does that constraint actually protect against?
Answer A second field implies remembered state — the previous destination, a "was gathering" flag, a saved schedule index — which is the schedule-rewriting design wearing a different hat. One nullable field is only sufficient because nothing needs restoring, so the field count is a proxy for the property that matters.
