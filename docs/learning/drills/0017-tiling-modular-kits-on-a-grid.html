Drill: Tiling Modular Kits over a Parametric Grid

1. Why not start tiling at the map edge?

The map spans ±110 and tiles are 16 wide. What breaks if you lay tiles edge-to-edge starting at -110?

Answer

220/16 = 13.75, and tile centers land at -102, -86, … — the junctions at ±32 fall mid-tile, so junction art can't be placed. Anchor the grid to the junctions (centers at multiples of 16) and treat the 6-unit leftovers at the edges as fitted end caps instead.

2. The 0.8-unit curb

The road tile is 2×2×0.1. Scaling it uniformly by 8 fills the 16-unit street — and creates what bug?

Answer

The 0.1 thickness becomes 0.8 world units: a knee-high asphalt platform over ground that collision says is flat, so players float/clip visually. Scale XZ from the bounds, pin Y to a small constant (0.05).

3. Junction double-draw

The junction tile at (32,32) belongs to both the x=32 street and the z=32 street. How does the loop avoid drawing it twice, and why would twice be bad?

Answer

Only the Z-running pass draws junction tiles; the X-running pass skips those centers. Two coplanar copies would z-fight (flickering markings), and a rotated second copy could disagree with the first's art.

4. Rotation and scale order

DrawModelEx scales in model space before rotating. For a 90°-yawed tile filling worldX×worldZ, which model axis gets worldZ?

Answer

Model X. After a 90° yaw the model's X axis lies along world Z, so scale.x = worldZ / size.x and scale.z = worldX / size.z — the axes swap whenever the rotation flag is set.

5. Where do zebra crossings go, and why there?

Answer

On the arm tiles directly beside each junction (centers ±16 and ±48 — distance 16 from a junction center). That's where the 48-unit sidewalk blocks end, so pedestrians naturally cross there; it's derived from the same block constants, not hand-placed coordinates.
