Drill 0032 — Side-Channel Leakage in Metadata

Answer before expanding. Source: lesson 0032, issue #200.

1. The model never confesses under pressure. Why was the secret still leaking?
Answer Through the [[MOOD:]] tag, which the model chooses on every reply and which drives the rendered face. Guilty Marge went angry on turn one where innocent Marge laughed. The system kept the secret in the channel it was watching, and lost it in one nobody designed as a channel.
2. Why is "the killer is never angry" the wrong pass condition?
Answer Suppressing affect entirely gives a cast of bland androids, and it optimises the wrong thing. The fix for a leak is to hide the signal in noise, not delete the signal.
3. How was the statistic derived, and why a rank?
Answer From the attack a player would actually run: ask everyone one question, sort the faces by hostility, accuse the top one. That is a rank, so the statistic is a rank. General rule: derive the metric from the exploit. If you cannot state the attack your metric detects in one sentence, it is measuring something adjacent.
4. Why a permutation test rather than a chi-square?
Answer No distributional assumption, no expected-count floor at these sample sizes, no new dependency, and the result restates in plain words a person can check — "five residents in ten look at least this hostile".
5. The first implementation had only the rank test and reported a clean PASS. What was it blind to?
Answer A rank collapses a whole distribution into one number, so it cannot see a killer conspicuous in some OTHER mood. Measuring the full mood distribution showed the issue's literal condition failing — killer vs secret-keepers TVD 0.59.
6. The literal pass condition failed but the verdict is not "leak". Explain the direction.
Answer The killer is separable from the secret-keepers because the killer reads ORDINARY — indistinguishable from the bystanders (TVD 0.12). It is the secret-keepers who stand out, at embarrassed on 59% of turns. The killer is buried in a group of six ordinary residents, so sorting faces does not find them.
7. So what IS broken, even though nothing leaks?
Answer The secret-keepers are not providing cover. They exist so "deflection is common and hostility stops being a unique signal" — cover requires the killer and the keepers to look alike, and they look nothing alike. The killer is defended by being unremarkable, not by being camouflaged among deflectors. That is a real defence that works, and it is not the one the role files describe.
8. The plan asked "how many secret-keepers is enough cover?". What did the data say?
Answer The count is the wrong lever. Eight keepers would deepen the embarrassed cluster without moving the killer any closer to it. The lever is the keepers' demeanour, which the model follows very literally and very consistently.
9. Why did the probe name two directions and take neither?
Answer Both are content decisions, not probe decisions. A measurement tool that also decides content is one you can no longer trust to report against its own preferences.
10. The killer contributes 8 turns. What must the report say about that?
Answer Report the n beside the verdict, and say which findings survive the noise. TVD at n=8 is noisy; the two runs moved killer-vs-keepers from 0.85 to 0.59. What survives both runs is the ORDERING (killer near bystanders, far from keepers, keepers far from bystanders), not the values.
11. Why must "FAIL" and "CANNOT EVALUATE" be different exit codes?
Answer A run that could not reach the model has measured nothing. Exit 0 PASS, 1 FAIL, 2 CANNOT EVALUATE. Collapsing the last two into a pass is the worst thing a gate can do; collapsing them into a failure sends someone debugging content when Ollama is simply down.
12. Why did the unimplemented scaffold return 2 rather than 0?
Answer So a scaffold can never report a pass. An unimplemented gate that exits 0 is indistinguishable from a passing one, and it will sit in a pipeline being trusted.
13. Name four side channels outside this codebase and the general question to ask.
Answer Latency (cache hit vs miss), response length ("I can't discuss that" is short), error codes ("wrong password" vs "no such user"), refusal style. The question is not "did it reveal the secret?" but "what differs between the case where there is a secret and the case where there is not?" Everything that differs is a channel.
14. Why must the probe render prompts through persona_prompt --state rather than assembling them in Python?
Answer The role block's PLACEMENT is what the probe measures — earlier placement was measured to corrupt the action protocol. A Python copy would have its own placement, so the probe would measure the copy rather than the game, and the result would look real.
