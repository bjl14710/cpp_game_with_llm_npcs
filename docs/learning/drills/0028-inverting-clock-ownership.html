Drill 0028 — Inverting a Clock's Ownership Without Disturbing Its Consumers

Answer before expanding. Source: lesson 0028, issue #155.

1. State the direction of causation in free roam and in a match, using the world hour as the subject of both sentences.
Answer Free roam: time passes (advanceTime(dt) moves the hour at a fixed scale) and everything else reacts to it. A match: the phase advances and the hour is computed from its progress — worldHour() sweeps dayStartHour → dayEndHour across the investigation. Same variable, opposite direction of causation.
2. Six systems read the world hour. Exactly how many of them changed in #155, and what property of their coupling is the reason?
Answer Zero. They are coupled to a value (they read world.state().timeOfDayHours()) rather than to a call — none subscribes to a tick, none calls advanceTime itself, none caches an hour across frames. Swapping the writer is therefore invisible to every reader.
3. Name three consumer designs that would each have made this change expensive, and say what each would have cost.
Answer (a) Event subscription (onHourChanged) — two publishers to reconcile plus ordering bugs when both fire. (b) Per-consumer timers (each holding its own float myHour_) — six independent drifts; the match could only ask nicely rather than drive. (c) Consumers calling advanceTime() themselves — six call sites to find, and the clock advancing six times per frame.
4. The issue demanded "exactly ONE owner of the clock — assert this rather than assuming it." Explain why a bool matchRunning plus two branches fails that requirement, and what was done instead.
Answer A flag is a second representation of a fact, so it can disagree with reality, and branches scattered where each was convenient invite a third call site that no future reader will notice. Instead both owners collapse into the single free function advanceWorldClock(state, match, dt): there is no other call site, and whether the std::optional<MatchClock> holds a value is the answer to "who owns the clock". One representation cannot drift from itself.
5. How was "free roam is byte-identical" actually established, and why does that beat reading the diff?
Answer The same deterministic capture — --frames 240 --camera 0 30 180 — was run against the pre-change binary and the post-change binary, and the two PNGs compared by md5 (5d4937aa85c299c292597e6dbc276dae both times). Fixed frame count plus fixed camera plus a deterministic world makes the artefact reproducible, which converts a judgement call ("looks the same to me") into a comparison that either holds or does not.
6. MatchPhase::Intro could have reused Resolution on "day zero" — same duration, same hold-the-clock behaviour. Give the argument against, in terms of downstream cost.
Answer Every switch on MatchPhase downstream (the vote, the plaza gather, the phase HUD) would have to distinguish a real Resolution from the fake day-zero one, and each would have to get that condition right independently. One enum value is far cheaper than the same condition repeated at every site. A phase that means something different should not borrow another's name.
7. Intro, Vote, Resolution and Ended all hold the clock rather than sweeping it. What does Intro hold it at, what do the others hold it at, and why is that difference the whole justification for the separate enum value?
Answer Intro holds at dayStartHour (09:00) — the day has not begun, and opening on a dusk sky would spend the diegetic countdown before the player has moved. Vote, Resolution and Ended hold at dayEndHour (20:00) — the day is spent, and letting the hour run during a slow vote would slide the town into full night and make the plaza gather unreadable. Sharing "hold the clock" while differing on which value to hold is exactly what a shared enum slot could not have expressed.
8. MatchRules::dayEndHour is 20.0 and DayNight's dusk band is 18.0→21.0. Name the feature that overlap delivers, and the test that protects it.
Answer The vote opens while the sky is visibly going orange, so the deadline is legible with no HUD element at all — the light is the countdown. Guarded by "the vote opens inside the dusk band, so the light is the countdown", which asserts dayEndHour lies strictly inside kDuskStart…kDuskEnd. Without it, a later retune of either constant silently costs the game its only UI-free signalling and nothing fails.
9. Written recall. A colleague wants to add a "slow motion" debug mode that halves the passage of time. Where does it belong, and what must it not do?
Answer It belongs at the single write site — scale dt on the way into advanceWorldClock, or scale the phase durations in MatchRules. It must not add a second place that writes setTimeOfDayHours, must not introduce a flag that duplicates "is a match running", and must not be applied inside any consumer — a consumer that scales its own copy of the hour re-creates precisely the per-consumer drift that made this change cheap to avoid.
